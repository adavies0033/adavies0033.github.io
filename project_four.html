My Not A Boring Competition Journey

My journey into the Not-A-Boring Competition began much like my earlier dive into motorsport with a chance encounter on the very same road at Monash University’s Clayton campus where I first met an Monash Motorsport member all those years ago. On this particular day, I was admist contemplation about which project to take on for my Honours year when I happened to run into an old friend from my early mathematics coursework. As fate would have it, he was now the acting CEO of a newly formed group within the Civil Engineering department, known as Monash BEST. After a short conversation about my interests and what I’d been working on, he suggested I take on a preliminary design study for the entire power system of their competition model. What began as a simple scoping task quickly expanded into something far more ambitious ultimately leading to the creation of what are now the Software and Electrical departments within Monash BEST, with myself as the founding head of both divisions.

The project began with me integrating myself into the existing team this being a somewhat acceltic group of aspirational engineers from various disciplines all united by a common goal of competing on the world stage in the annual Not-A-Boring competition. stduents from various backgrounds with independent systems and directions in which they wanted to take machine. After a prolonged period of familiarisation with the existing design, I realised quickly that the system must be greatly expanded and formalised in order to faciliate the end funcitonality of the Machione. Matured systesm are 100kW powertrains with complex energy management systems and high voltaage drives and control systems. Ontop of this as the team was newly established both reasources and expeterise was low. Over the course of several months under consultation with the C-suite of Monash BEST and various industry partner and academic faculty I formalised design and doccumentation and lead the direction to undertaking a ZOnal ECU controls achitecture with a central high voltage DC link and distributed power electronics modules. This architecture would go on to form the basis of the current Monash BEST vehicle now in development.
