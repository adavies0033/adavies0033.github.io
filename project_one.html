Reflection on my FSAE Monash Motorsport Journey

10/11/2025

Autonomous Systems Recruitment and Development

Recommencing after my first year undergraduate engineering studies at Monash University I had found myself pursuing a role which could foster my interests in AI and Machine Learning. After attending several of the Monash Deep Neuron (MDN) networking events I felt primed to apply for a role with the group. As fate would have it, during the morning of my application to MDN an unexpected meeting a Monash Motorsport recruiter changed my trajectory to the newly established Autonomous Systems sub-team within Monash Motorsport (MMS). After undergoing the recruitment process for the team I found myself amoungst the junior cohort of the team, many of whom I consider close friends to this day. Throughout the next 3 months we underwent rapid upskilling developing an C++ based path planning and motion control algorithm for which operated in the team's autonomous pipeline and rviz simulation environment. The delivery of the project gave me my first taste of working in a high performing engineering team, with late nights in the workshop, and several timed deadlines to meet. The experience was invaluable in developing my technical and interpersonal skills, and set up the foundation of work on the M19-D showcase vehicle post the conclusion of the prohibitionary project and junior presentation night to both current serving members and alumni to the team.

Figure 1: CAD model of the MBEST Micro-TBM system.

Technical Scope

  • Design and modelling of a 10 kW induction motor drive system for cutterhead actuation.
  • High-voltage DC bus architecture and LV power distribution to embedded controllers.
  • Simulation of power, torque, and thermal performance using MATLAB/Simulink and ANSYS.
  • Communication platform for telemetry, control signals, and subsystem coordination.
Figure 2: System-level architecture showing power and communication subsystems.

Key Challenges

The project had to balance NaBC constraints, physical envelope limitations, thermal performance, and safety requirements, while remaining modular enough to support future upgrades in power level and autonomy.

Figure 3: Prototype testing of motor drive and telemetry link.

Outcomes

The final design demonstrates a viable power and communication platform for a competition-scale TBM, with validated motor operation, controllable propulsion concepts, and a hardware architecture that can be extended for higher power and increased autonomy in future work.
